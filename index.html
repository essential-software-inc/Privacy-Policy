Bluebook Privacy Policy

Effective 6 October 2026

Bluebook is published by Essential Software (“we”, “us”, and “our” in this policy), and we're glad you're here. This page explains in plain language what the app stores, where it lives, and what leaves your device.

On-device storage

Your pages, drafts, images, favourites, preferences and app settings all stay on your device. There's one exception: when you're signed in, we keep track of which stories you've opened on our servers, under your account (see "Stories, subscriptions & likes" below). For each account signed in on the device, the app also keeps a copy of which notes you've liked and which accounts you follow (signing out clears the follow copy). It keeps a short queue of any likes or follows you tapped while offline that haven't been sent yet. It also keeps a small number that tells it how far your device's clock is off from our servers, so those taps get the right time. If you sign in with Google, your notes, along with your favorites and theme choice, are also backed up to our servers (see below).

Google sign-in & sync

Signing in is totally optional, and Google handles it, so we never see your Google password. We get your Google account ID and email address, and we keep them along with your sign-up date, your last sign-in date, the last time the app was active on your account (see "Activity counts" below), and a code that keeps you signed in (a session lasts up to 30 days). While you're signed in, your notes (including embedded images), your favorites and your theme choice are backed up to our servers so you can restore them on another device. Your Pro purchase (if you have one) and any ad view-credit balance (see "In-app purchases" below) are linked to your account too, so they come with you. Signing out stops syncing. Your backup stays put, so you can pick it up again when you sign back in, and deleting your account removes it, as described below. If an image in your backup isn't used by any note any more (for example, after you delete a note or swap an image), the nightly clean-up deletes it once it's more than 14 days old.

Your account is yours to keep or remove. You can delete it any time by tapping the account icon and choosing Delete account under Show more. That signs this device out and schedules your account for deletion in 30 days. The deletion happens during our nightly clean-up, so it can land up to a day after the 30 days are up. When it does, we permanently delete your account record, synced notes and images, profile name and picture, subscriptions (the ones you made and the ones to you), records of the stories you've opened, your likes and follows (including the times we noted for them), running ads and their view statistics, your invite code and any invites you made or used, your ad access record, your alerts (including ones you deleted from your list), and any unspent ad view-credit balance. We also take down every page you published (we keep a copy of each page, along with its record, for about 30 more days, as described under "Publishing a page"). Until then, your published pages and stories stay online. Changed your mind? Sign back in with the same Google account during the 30 days and the deletion is cancelled, with full access restored. Every device where you're signed in gets signed out as part of the deletion (each one finds out the next time the app contacts our server, usually within about a minute). Deleting your account doesn't delete anything held by Google or RevenueCat. Their own policies cover that.

In-app purchases

Unlocking Pro and buying ad view-credit packages are both purchases made through Google Play Billing. RevenueCat, a third-party subscription service, handles them for us. RevenueCat gets the purchase details it needs to confirm what you bought, linked to an ID. Before you sign in, that's an anonymous ID RevenueCat makes for your install. Once you sign in, it's your Google account ID, which is what lets a purchase follow you to another device. After that, the two work differently. The app checks directly with RevenueCat (not through our servers) to see whether Pro is active. It keeps a saved copy of that answer on your device, and refreshes it from RevenueCat when the app launches and after a purchase or restore. Ad view-credits do go through our servers. They hear from RevenueCat (not from Google directly) that you bought views, and add the views to your account. We keep a list of your view credits: each purchase (for a particular ad, or added to your balance from Settings), each time views are put on an ad (with that ad's address), each refund or return, and each gift of free views for an invite, all with the time. Views returned to you stay in your balance, and you can put them on an ad without buying again. That doesn't involve Google Play or RevenueCat. If Google Play refunds a purchase, we take those views back. That list is deleted with your account. We never get your payment details, because Google handles those directly. See RevenueCat's own privacy policy for how it handles what it receives.

Publishing a page

Publishing is always your choice, and it only happens when you tap Publish. We receive your page's content, its title, description and tags (if you've added them), when the note was created, and the address you choose, and we store them so the page can be shown at a public link. Anyone with that link can view the page. You can publish without signing in, and then the page isn't linked to any account. If you later sign in and, using that page's private token on your device, turn on "Show in stories" or save an update to it, we link the page to your account from then on. It then shows up among your published pages, counts toward your account's page limit, and is taken down if you delete your account. We also give you a private token for each page. It's stored on your device, and we don't keep it in its original form. We only keep a scrambled version, just enough to check that it matches when you send it back to update or unpublish. Updating a published page sends the new content the same way. Unpublishing takes the page down, and if it was running as an ad, the ad stops too. We may keep a copy for about 30 days afterward (our clean-up runs nightly), in case of an abuse report or takedown request, and then delete it. If the page was reported as child sexual abuse material, we keep the copy for 18 months instead (see "Reporting a published page"). The page's own record is kept for the same time and then deleted. It holds the address, a scrambled form of the private token, the title, description and tags, and, if the page was linked to an account, that account's internal ID. That's why it stays even after the account itself is deleted. If we remove a page because of a takedown request or abuse report, its address is reserved permanently. To keep that reservation in place, we keep a small record for as long as the address stays reserved: the address, a scrambled form of the private token, the title, description and tags, and, if the page was linked to an account, that account's internal ID. This record stays even after the copy or the account is deleted, because deleting it would lift the reservation. Publishing also briefly counts requests from your IP address to limit spam. That count sits in a short-lived counter that disappears on its own after about a minute, and it isn't kept afterward or linked to your account or the page. Separately, to stop spam, we also count how often you like, subscribe, mark stories as seen, add an invite code or back up. That count is linked to your account and disappears within a couple of minutes.

Publishing as an ad

Want more people to see a page? From the Publish sheet's "Publish as Ad" tab, you can turn a page you've already published into an ad. Other people using the app see it as a starting template when they start a new note in Write (or in Share, for a Share note). It isn't just for people who have the page's link, like a regular published page. You need to sign in to run an ad. Views are bought as described under "In-app purchases" above, and once the purchase is confirmed, we add those views to your account. We also save how many views an ad has used and its status (running, used up, or taken down). To tell you how many different people an ad reached, we save an anonymous ID that each device makes, keeps, and sends whenever a view is counted, whether or not the viewer is signed in. It isn't linked to a Google account, email address, or anything else that identifies someone. We use it to tell whether a repeat view of an ad came from a device we already counted, and for the activity counts described under "Activity counts" below. See "Analytics" below too. When you unpublish an ad, it disappears from Write and Share right away, and any views it had left go unused. That balance isn't returned to your account. The page itself and its normal link stay as they were, and taking the page down is a separate step from the Publish to Web tab. "External images & embeds" below explains how images, video, audio and social posts inside an ad are loaded. Ads can only use images from Wikipedia or Wikimedia Commons, and video, audio and social posts added with the editor's own tools (YouTube, Spotify, Instagram, X, or Streamable).

Ad access & invites

Some bigger ad packages have to be earned. To work out which packages you can start and how many ads you can run at once, our servers count these things, using the records described in this policy: your subscribers, published notes, stories, likes you've received and given, accounts you follow, how many of the last 30 days your account was active, your running ads, and any strikes. A strike comes from an alert we sent you about a removal, pause or suspension. We keep those alerts for up to a year, but a strike only counts for 60 days, or until we excuse it. You see these counts as your progress. We use them to enforce the limits, and other people never see them.

Every signed-in account gets a random 8-character invite code, saved with the account. When someone adds a code, we save a record of who entered whose code, when it happened, and whether and why it stopped counting (the invited account was suspended or being deleted, or we removed it for abuse). A friend can add one code, in their first 14 days. You get an alert when a friend joins, but it doesn't say who. You only see totals: how many friends added your code and how many count right now, never who. An invite counts once your friend has published a note and been active on at least two days. If your friend chooses to subscribe at the same time, that's saved like any other subscription. When you share your code, it goes through your device's share sheet or clipboard, so the message is sent by the app you pick and we never see it. When an invite counts, we add 100 free views to your balance. We add it to the list of your view credits, together with the invited account's internal ID, so the same invite can't be rewarded twice. That entry stays in that list (and only there) until you delete your account, even if your friend's account is deleted first. If either account is deleted, the record of the invite itself is deleted.

Reporting a published page

Reports help keep Bluebook a good place for everyone. Anyone can report a published page from its public link. To stop spam, we count how many reports come from an IP address. That count lives in a short-lived counter that disappears on its own about a minute after the last report from that address. It's never saved as part of the report. We only keep the reason and details that were sent, which page it's about, and when. A report doesn't record who sent it. A report stays on file until someone reviews it, even if the page is unpublished or its author's account is deleted in the meantime. After it's handled, it's deleted. The exception is reports of child sexual abuse material. We keep those for 18 months, along with a copy of the reported page. The report's 18 months start when it's closed. The page copy's 18 months start when it was reported, or when it was most recently reported if it's reported again. The same period covers any other copy we hold of that page. We may also send a notification with the page's address, the reason and the details to our private alerts channel. We handle reports of child sexual abuse material the way the law requires, including passing them to the proper authority. We keep a short record of each one we pass on (the page's address, the date, the authority's reference for it, and a brief note) for 18 months from the latest. When we take an action (like a takedown, a dismissed report, or a restored page), we write down the action, the page's address, the time, and a short note such as the reason given. We keep that for about 13 months, as a record of what was done.

Pages running as ads work the same way. An ad and its published page are exactly the same thing at the same web address, so there's only one way to report either: go to that page's address and use the report link there. There's no separate process for ads.

Right now, the app doesn't show you that address when an ad appears as a starting point on the Write or Share screen. To find it, save the ad without adding anything new, then open the Publish sheet for that note. It fills in the ad's own web address automatically. Open that address in a browser and you'll see the report link.

Alerts

We want you to know what's happening with your account and the app. When one of your pages is reported, or we remove or pause something of yours, or change your account's status, you'll get an automatic message (an alert) that says why. We also use alerts for good news and updates, like announcements, new features, a bigger ad package unlocking, a friend adding your invite code, planned maintenance, safety tips, or a change to this policy or our terms. They can go to every account or to specific ones, and they're saved, shown and deleted the same way as the others. Alerts show up under Alerts in your library, which only appears once your account has at least one. A number on it shows how many have arrived since you last looked at the list, and the list's title shows how many you have in total. We save the text of each alert (which can name the page or item it's about), when it was sent, and when you deleted it. We also save one marker on your account for the newest alert you've been shown, so the count follows you across your devices. Deleting an alert only removes it from your list. We keep the record, including deleted ones, for up to a year so we can review what was sent to an account. An alert never includes who reported, or the details they wrote, because we don't pass those on. If you delete your account, your alerts and that marker are deleted too.

External images & embeds

Images you add to a page by URL are loaded straight from their source when the page is built. Instagram, X, Streamable, YouTube and Spotify embeds load straight from those services. We have nothing to do with these third-party hosts, with one exception: when you paste an Instagram share link or an X (t.co) short link using the editor's own tools, our server follows the link just to find out which post it points to. It doesn't read the page. It also counts requests from your IP address in a short-lived counter that disappears on its own after about a minute, and that isn't kept afterward or linked to your account. A page published as an ad can only use images linked directly from Wikipedia or Wikimedia Commons, and can't contain images uploaded from your device. We check this automatically before an ad can go live or be updated.

Linked pages

This section is about links inside the app. In preview, tapping a link in a page shows its address in a sheet with a copy option. The app doesn't open or load the linked page. Nothing is fetched, and we can't see what links a page contains. A published page works a bit differently, depending on how you're viewing it. If someone opens its public web address directly, outside the app, they're looking at a plain web page in their own browser, so links inside it are ordinary clickable links that open like any other link on the web. Inside the app, opening a story or a note you found through search shows that same published page in the app's own built-in viewer (see "Stories, subscriptions & likes" below), so you don't leave the app to see it. A link you tap inside that viewer opens in your own browser, which takes you out of the app. The app doesn't load the linked page itself, and we have nothing to do with that site, but like any site you open in a browser, it can see your IP address. If the app can't open the link, it copies the address instead.

Stories, subscriptions & likes

From a published note's Publish sheet, you can turn on "Show in stories" (you need to sign in). That adds the note to a stories strip that other people using the app can see, not only people who have the link. It's similar to Publish as Ad, but without purchased views. Our servers save the note's web address, title, description and tags (if you've added them), when the note was created, and up to three preview images from the note's published page (plus how many images the note has, so a feed card can show how many more there are), along with your account ID. Your story shows the title. The Subscribed feed of anyone who subscribes to you shows the title, description, tags, how long ago the note was created, and how many likes it has. Preview images that come from your device are copied to our servers, and they can be loaded without a sign-in check while the story is showing. A preview image added by URL isn't copied, so viewers' devices load it straight from its source when they see your story or feed card, and that host can see their IP address. If you turn the toggle off within the first 24 hours, or unpublish the note at any time, we delete the saved copies, including the saved description and tags. Other people's devices never get your Google account ID or email. Instead, each account has a separate random author ID that means nothing outside the app. It's sent with your stories, so your picture and display name can go with them and a Subscribe tap can reach the right account. We make it ourselves, it isn't made from your Google account, and it's deleted with your account. To tell you apart from other authors, viewers see your display name, picture, or the note's title. A story can appear in the strip for 24 hours from when you turn on "Show in stories" (editing the note doesn't restart that clock). After that, it leaves the strip and your Stories tab. The strip isn't a list of every story. It shows a selection of other people's stories that reshuffles every few hours, so a story still within its 24 hours may or may not be shown to a given viewer. Your own live stories always lead your own strip, and stories from accounts a viewer already follows show up in their Subscribed feed instead of the strip. A story stays in the Subscribed feed of people who follow you (from when they followed you onward) for 7 days from when you turned on "Show in stories", or until you unpublish the note, whichever comes first. After that it leaves the feed, but the published page itself stays online. After the first 24 hours the toggle turns itself off. That just tells you the story has left the strip and your Stories tab. It stays in your followers' feeds for the rest of the 7 days. If you turn the toggle off yourself within the first 24 hours, the story disappears from the strip and from your followers' feeds.

A display name and profile picture are both optional. If you set a display name, it's shown on your stories and, to anyone who subscribes to you, on your notes in their Subscribed feed. If you leave it blank, viewers just see the note's title in both places. A display name can be up to 30 characters. If you set a profile picture, it's only shown on your stories, as the ring around your avatar in the stories strip. It doesn't show on your notes in the Subscribed feed. A picture you upload is stored on our servers and served from a web address based on your random author ID. That address isn't behind a sign-in check, but it only serves your picture while you have at least one story showing (or to you, when you're signed in). So anyone who has your author ID, for example from seeing one of your stories, can load it during that time, and no one else can. Once you have no stories showing, the picture is no longer shown to others. If you remove the picture, it's deleted from our servers. With no picture, the story ring uses the note's own image if it has one, and only then an initial letter, taken from your display name (or the note's title if you haven't set one). That initial is never taken from your account email, which is never sent to other viewers at all.

A signed-in account can subscribe to (follow) another account from that account's story. We save who follows whom so we can build your Subscribed feed, show whether you're already following someone, and keep your follows on every device you sign in on. You can see how many accounts follow you as a total, never who, and how many accounts you follow. The app also keeps a copy of who you follow on your device, so it can show the Subscribe/Subscribed button without asking our servers each time. We also record, under your account, which stories you've opened, so a story you've already seen doesn't keep grabbing your attention in the strip. Searching your Subscribed feed happens on your device, within the stories already loaded. What you type is never sent to us. A signed-in account can also like a published note. We record, under your account, which notes you've liked so the heart shows correctly across your devices. For each note you like or unlike, and each account you follow or unfollow, we also note the time of your most recent action. That way, taps made on different devices (or offline and sent later) are applied in the right order. That note stays after you unlike or unfollow, and is removed once the like or follow is gone and the note is more than 30 days old, or when your account is deleted. Each published note's own like count shows on its card wherever that card appears: your Published and Stories tabs, and the Subscribed feed or Liked tab of anyone who sees the note, but never who liked it. You can also see one total on your account screen: the number of likes across all your published notes. If you unpublish a note, the likes it received are removed, so both that note's count and the combined total go down. When a published note that isn't showing as a story appears as a card, either in its author's Published tab or in someone's Liked tab, we may copy up to three preview images from that page to our servers. Like story preview images, these copies can be loaded without a sign-in check, and they're deleted when the note is unpublished or its author's account is deleted.

Third-party requests & server logs

Using the app makes a few requests to other companies, which can see your IP address, like any website you visit. A script library (three.js) is loaded from Cloudflare's cdnjs when the app opens. Google's sign-in code is loaded from Google when you sign in. The app sometimes asks Google (gstatic.com) for a tiny blank page, just to check whether you're online. And if you paste a Spotify link using the editor's own tools, the app asks Spotify (open.spotify.com) for that link's embed details. Requests to our servers run on Cloudflare, which processes them and may keep standard request logs (like the time, the address requested, and your IP address) that we can use to run and fix the service. We don't use them for analytics or tracking.

Activity counts

We'd like to know roughly how many people use the app, and how many are using it right now, so our servers note when the app is open. While it's open, the app contacts our server when it launches, when you come back to it, and about every four minutes, sending the same anonymous device ID described under "Publishing as an ad" above. If you're signed in, this (and any other request you make while signed in) saves only the time your account was last active, on your account. If you're not signed in, the server saves the device ID with the times it was first and last seen (plus the daily log described below), and nothing else. It isn't linked to a name, email address or Google account, and if you later sign in on that device, the signed-out record is deleted. We use these records for private, overall totals (like total, new and currently active users and devices), to look up an account's last activity when we're running the service, and, for signed-in accounts, to count recent active days toward ad access and invites (see "Ad access & invites"). They're never shown to other people, shared, or used for advertising. The last-activity record is deleted about 90 days after the last activity (an account's is also deleted with the account). Separately, so usage can be charted across weeks, months and years, the server keeps a minimal daily log. For each day an account or device was active, it holds one entry with just that date and the account or device ID, with no name, email or content. We use these entries for those overall charts and for the active-day counts in "Ad access & invites", and they're removed automatically after 800 days (about 26 months). They aren't removed early when you delete your account or sign in on a device that was signed out. There's no setting to turn this off, because we don't collect anything beyond this. If you clear the app's storage, you get a fresh anonymous ID from then on.

Analytics

We do not collect usage tracking or crash reports from this app, and do not track what you do inside it. The only usage figures it keeps are the two uses of the anonymous device ID described above: counting an ad's unique viewers (see "Publishing as an ad"), and the activity counts (see "Activity counts").
