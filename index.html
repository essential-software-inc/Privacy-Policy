Bluebook Privacy Policy

Effective 3 October 2026

Bluebook is published by Essential Software (“we”, “us”, and “our” in this policy), and we're glad you're here. This page explains in plain language what the app stores, where it lives, and what leaves your device.

On-device storage

Your pages, drafts, images, favourites, preferences, and UI state all live on your device, with one exception: when you're signed in, which stories you've opened is tracked on our servers, against your account, instead of on your device (see "Stories, subscriptions & likes" below). For each account signed in on the device, the app also keeps a copy of which notes you've liked and which accounts you follow (signing out clears the follow copy), a short queue of any likes or follows you tapped while offline that haven't been sent yet, and a small number recording how far your device's clock is off from our servers, so those taps are time-stamped correctly. If you sign in with Google, your notes, along with your favorites and theme choice, are additionally backed up to our servers (see below).

Google sign-in & sync

Signing in is entirely optional, and Google handles it, so we never see your Google password. We receive your Google account identifier and email address, and keep them with your sign-up date, your last sign-in date, the last time the app was active on your account (see "Activity counts" below), and a session token that keeps you signed in (a session lasts up to 30 days). While signed in, your notes (including embedded images), along with which notes you've marked as favorites and your theme choice, are backed up to our servers so they can be restored on another device, and your Pro purchase (if any) and any ad view-credit balance (see "In-app purchases" below) are linked to your account so they carry over too. Signing out stops further syncing. Your backup stays in place so you can pick it up again when you sign back in, and deleting your account removes it, as described below. Images in your backup that no note references any more (for example after you delete a note or replace an image) are deleted by the nightly clean-up once they are more than 14 days old.

Your account is yours to keep or remove: you can delete it at any time by tapping the account icon and choosing Delete account under Show more. Doing so signs this device out and schedules your account for deletion in 30 days. The deletion runs as part of a nightly clean-up, so it can land up to a day after the 30 days are up. When it does, we permanently delete your account record, synced notes and images, profile name and picture, subscriptions (both ones you made and ones to you), story-seen, like and follow records (including the timestamps kept for them), running ads and their view statistics, your alerts (including ones you deleted from your list), and any unspent ad view-credit balance, and take down every page you published (a copy of each page, along with its record, is then kept for about 30 more days, as described under "Publishing a page"). Until then your published pages and stories stay online. Changed your mind? Signing back in with the same Google account during the 30 days cancels the deletion and restores full access. Every device where you're signed in is signed out as part of the deletion (each one finds out the next time the app contacts the server, usually within about a minute). Deleting your account does not delete anything held by Google or RevenueCat, which is governed by their own policies.

In-app purchases

Unlocking Pro and buying ad view-credit packages are both purchases made through Google Play Billing, processed on our behalf by RevenueCat, a third-party subscription-management service. RevenueCat receives the purchase and entitlement data needed to confirm what you bought, tied to an ID. Before you sign in, that's an anonymous ID RevenueCat generates for your install, and once you sign in, it's your Google account identifier, which is what lets a purchase be carried over to another device. The two unlocks work differently after that: whether Pro is active is checked directly against RevenueCat by the app itself, not through our own servers. The app keeps a cached copy of that result on your device and refreshes it from RevenueCat on launch and after a purchase or restore. Ad view-credits do involve our servers, which receive confirmation from RevenueCat, not from Google directly, and credit the views to your account. We never receive your payment details, which Google handles directly. See RevenueCat's own privacy policy for how it handles what it receives.

Publishing a page

Publishing is always your choice, and it only happens when you tap Publish. Your page's content, its title, description and tags (if you've added them), when the note was created, and the address you choose are sent to us and stored so the page can be served at a public link. Anyone with that link can view the page. You can publish without signing in, in which case the page isn't linked to any account. If you later sign in and, using that page's private token on your device, turn on "Show in stories" or save an update to it, we link the page to your account from then on, so it appears among your published pages, counts toward your account's page limit, and is taken down if you delete your account. We also give you a private token for that page. It's stored on your device, not kept as-is on our side. Only a scrambled version of it is kept there, just enough to confirm it matches when you send it back to update or unpublish. Updating a published page sends the new content the same way. Unpublishing takes it down, and if the page was running as an ad, the ad stops too. A copy may be kept for about 30 days afterward (our clean-up runs nightly) in case of an abuse report or takedown request, then deleted (18 months if the page was reported as child sexual abuse material; see "Reporting a published page"). The page's own record is kept for the same period and then deleted. It holds the address, a scrambled form of the private token, the title, description and tags, and, if the page was tied to an account, that account's internal ID, which is why it stays even after the account itself has been deleted. If we remove a page for a takedown request or abuse report, its address is reserved permanently. To keep that reservation in place, we keep a small record for as long as that address stays reserved: the address, a scrambled form of the private token, the title, description and tags, and, if the page was tied to an account, that account's internal ID. This record stays even after the copy or the account is deleted, since removing it would lift the reservation. Publishing also briefly counts requests from your IP address to limit spam. The address sits in a short-lived counter that expires on its own after about a minute, and it isn't kept afterward or linked to your account or the page. Separately, likes, subscribing, marking stories as seen, and backups are rate-limited with a short-lived counter tied to your account, which expires within a couple of minutes.

Publishing as an ad

From the Publish sheet's "Publish as Ad" tab, you can additionally register an already-published page to run as an ad, shown to other people using this app as a starting template when they tap Create, not only to people who have the page's link, which is how a regular published page works. Running an ad requires signing in. Buying views is a purchase handled as described under "In-app purchases" above; once confirmed, we credit the corresponding number of views to your account. We also store how many views an ad has used and its current status (running, used up, or taken down). To report how many unique viewers an ad has reached, alongside its total view count, we store an anonymous identifier that each device generates, keeps, and sends whenever a view is counted, whether or not the viewer is signed in. It isn't linked to a Google account, email address, or any other identifying information, and is used to tell whether a repeat view of a given ad came from a device already counted toward that ad, and for the activity counts described under "Activity counts" below; see also "Analytics" below. Unpublishing an ad removes it from Create immediately, and any views it had left go unused. That balance isn't returned to your account. The underlying published page and its normal link are unaffected by unpublishing an ad; taking the page itself down is still done separately from the Publish to Web tab. See "External images & embeds" below for how images, video, audio, and social-post embeds inside an ad are fetched. Ads are additionally restricted to images from Wikipedia or Wikimedia Commons, and to video, audio, and social-post embeds added through the editor's own tools (YouTube, Instagram, or Spotify).

Reporting a published page

Reports help keep Bluebook a good place for everyone. Anyone can report a published page from its public link. Reporting counts requests from the reporter's IP address for about a minute, just to stop spam. The address sits in a short-lived counter that expires on its own, and it's never stored as part of the report itself. We keep only the reason and details submitted, along with which page it's about and when. A report doesn't record who sent it. A report stays on file until it's reviewed, even if the page is unpublished or its author's account is deleted in the meantime. Once handled, it's deleted, except reports of child sexual abuse material, which are kept for 18 months, along with a copy of the reported page. The report's 18 months run from when it is closed; the page copy's run from when it was reported, or from the most recent report of that page if it's reported again, and the same period covers any other copy we hold of that page. A notification with the page's address, the reason, and the details may also be forwarded to our private alerting channel. Reports of child sexual abuse material are handled as the law requires, including being passed to the proper authority. We keep a short record of each one we pass on (the page's address, the date, the authority's reference for it, and a brief note) for 18 months from the latest. Moderation actions we take (such as a takedown, a dismissed report, or a restored page) are logged with the action, the page's address, the time, and a short note such as the reason given, for about 13 months, as a record of what was done.

This applies the same way to pages currently running as ads. An ad and its published page are the exact same thing at the exact same web address, so there's only one way to report either: go to that page's address and use the report link there. There's no separate process for ads.

Right now, the app doesn't show you that address when an ad appears as a starting point on the Create screen. To find it, save the ad without adding anything new to it, then open the Publish sheet for that note, which fills in the ad's own web address automatically. Open that address in a browser and you'll see the report link.

Alerts

We want you to know what's going on with your account and the app. When one of your pages is reported, or we remove or pause something of yours or change your account's status, you'll get an automatic message (an alert) stating the reason. We also use alerts to share news and good things with you, like announcements, new features, planned maintenance, safety tips, or a change to this policy or our terms. These can go to all accounts or to specific ones, and they're stored, shown, and deleted exactly like the others. Alerts appear under Alerts in your library, which only shows once your account has at least one. A count on it shows how many have arrived since you last viewed the list, and the list's own title shows how many you have in total. We store the text of each alert (which can name the page or item concerned), when it was sent, and when you deleted it, plus a single timestamp against your account marking the newest alert you've been shown, which is what lets that count follow you across your devices. Deleting an alert removes it from your list only; we keep the record, including deleted ones, for up to a year so we can review what was sent to an account. An alert never includes the reporter's identity or the details they wrote, which we don't pass on. If you delete your account, your alerts and that timestamp are deleted with it.

External images & embeds

Images embedded in a page via URL are fetched directly from their source when the page is built. YouTube, Instagram, and Spotify embeds load directly from those services. We have no involvement with these third-party hosts. A page published as an ad is additionally restricted to images linked directly from Wikipedia or Wikimedia Commons, and cannot contain images uploaded from your device. This is checked automatically before an ad can go live or be updated.

Linked pages

This section covers links inside the app. In preview, tapping a link in a page shows its address in a sheet with a copy option. The app does not open, load, or navigate to the linked page. Nothing is fetched, and we have no visibility into what links a page contains. A published page works differently, and how you're viewing it matters: someone who opens its public web address directly, outside the app, is looking at a plain web page in their own browser, so links inside it are ordinary clickable links that open like any other link on the web. Within the app itself, opening a story or a note found through search shows that same published page in-app, in the app's own built-in viewer (see "Stories, subscriptions & likes" below). You don't leave the app to see it. A link tapped inside that in-app view opens in your own browser, which takes you out of the app. The app doesn't load the linked page itself, and we have no involvement with that site, but like any site you open in a browser, it can see your IP address. If the app can't open the link, it copies its address instead.

Stories, subscriptions & likes

From a published note's Publish sheet, you can turn on "Show in stories" (this requires signing in). Doing so adds the note to a stories strip shown to other people using the app, not only to people who have the link, similar to how Publish as Ad works, but without purchased views. Our servers store the note's web address, title, description and tags (if you've added them), when the note was created, and up to three preview images extracted from the note's published page (plus how many images the note has, so a feed card can show how many more there are), along with your account identifier. The title is shown on your story; the title, description, tags, how long ago the note was created, and how many likes it has are shown on the note's card in the Subscribed feed of anyone who subscribes to you. Preview images that come from your device are copied to our servers and can be loaded without a sign-in check while the story is showing; a preview image added by URL isn't copied, so viewers' devices load it directly from its source when they see your story or feed card, and that host can see their IP address. Turning the toggle off within the first 24 hours, or unpublishing the note at any time, deletes the stored copies, including the stored description and tags. Other people's devices never receive your Google account identifier or email. Instead, each account is given a separate random author ID that means nothing outside the app; it's sent with your stories so your picture and display name can be tied to them and a Subscribe tap can reach the right account. It's generated by us, isn't derived from your Google account, and is deleted with your account. What a viewer sees to tell you apart from another author is your display name, picture, or the note's title. A story is eligible to appear in the strip for 24 hours from when you turn on "Show in stories" (editing the note doesn't restart that clock), after which it leaves the strip and your Stories tab. The strip isn't a list of every story: it shows a selection of other people's stories that reshuffles every few hours, so a story still within its 24 hours may or may not be shown to a given viewer. Your own live stories always lead your own strip, and stories from accounts a viewer already follows appear in their Subscribed feed instead of the strip. A story stays in the Subscribed feed of people who follow you (from when they followed you onward) for 7 days from when you turned on "Show in stories", or until you unpublish the note, whichever comes first; after that it leaves the feed, though the published page itself stays online. After the first 24 hours the toggle turns itself off. That just tells you the story has left the strip and your Stories tab; it stays in your followers' feeds for the rest of the 7 days. If you turn the toggle off yourself within the first 24 hours, the story disappears from the strip and from your followers' feeds.

A display name and profile picture are both optional. A display name, if set, is shown to other people on your stories and, to anyone who subscribes to you, on your notes in their Subscribed feed; leaving it blank means viewers just see the note's title instead, in both places. A display name is capped at 30 characters. A profile picture, if set, is shown only on your stories, as the ring avatar in the stories strip. It does not appear on your notes in the Subscribed feed. A profile picture you upload is stored on our servers and served back from a web address keyed to your random author ID. That address isn't behind a sign-in check, but it only serves your picture while you have at least one story showing (or to you, when you're signed in), so anyone who has your author ID, for instance from seeing one of your stories, can load it during that time, and no one else can. Once you have no stories showing, the picture is no longer served to others. Removing the picture deletes it from our servers; leaving it unset (or removing it) means the story ring falls back first to the note's own image if it has one, and only then to an initial letter, taken from your display name, or the note's title if that's unset too. That initial is never taken from your account email, which is never sent to other viewers at all.

A signed-in account can subscribe to (follow) another account from that account's story. We store who follows whom to build your Subscribed feed, to show whether you're already following an account, and to keep your follows on any device you sign in on. You can see how many accounts follow you, as a total, not who; the app also keeps a local copy of who you follow on your device to show the Subscribe/Subscribed button without a round trip. We also record, tied to your account, which stories you've opened, so a story you've already seen doesn't keep drawing your attention in the strip. Searching your Subscribed feed happens on your device, against the stories already loaded; what you type is not sent to us. A signed-in account can also like a published note; we record, tied to your account, which notes you've liked so the heart shows correctly across your devices. For each note you like or unlike, and each account you follow or unfollow, it also keeps the time of your most recent such action, so that taps made on different devices (or offline and sent later) are applied in the right order; that timestamp remains after you unlike or unfollow and is removed once the like or follow is gone and the timestamp is older than 30 days, or when your account is deleted. Each published note's own like count is shown on its card wherever that card appears: your Published and Stories tabs, and the Subscribed feed or Liked tab of anyone who sees the note, but never who liked it. You can also see one combined total, the number of likes across all your published notes, on your account screen. Unpublishing a note removes the likes it received, so both that note's count and the combined total go down accordingly. When a published note that isn't showing as a story appears as a card, either in its author's Published tab or in someone's Liked tab, we may copy up to three preview images from that page to our servers. Like story preview images, these copies can be loaded without a sign-in check, and they are deleted when the note is unpublished or its author's account is deleted.

Third-party requests & server logs

Using the app makes some requests to third parties, which see your IP address like any website you visit: a script library (three.js) is loaded from Cloudflare's cdnjs when the app opens; Google's sign-in code is loaded from Google when you sign in; and the app occasionally requests a tiny blank page from Google (gstatic.com) just to check whether you're online. Requests to our servers run on Cloudflare, which processes them and may keep standard request logs (such as time, address requested, and IP address) that we can use to operate and debug the service. We don't use them for analytics or tracking.

Activity counts

To know roughly how many people use the app, and how many are using it right now, our servers record when the app is open. While the app is open, it contacts the server on launch, when you return to it, and about every four minutes, sending the same anonymous per-device identifier described under "Publishing as an ad" above. If you're signed in, this (and any other request you make while signed in) records only the time your account was last active, against your account. If you're not signed in, the server records the device identifier with the times it was first and last seen (plus the daily log described below), and nothing else: it isn't linked to a name, email address, or Google account, and if you later sign in on that device the signed-out record is deleted. We use these records only for private, aggregate totals (such as total, new and currently active users and devices) and to look up an account's last activity when operating the service. They're never shown to other people, shared, or used for advertising, and that last-activity record is deleted about 90 days after its last activity (an account's is also deleted with the account). Separately, so usage can be charted across weeks, months and years, the server keeps a minimal daily log: for each day an account or device was active, one entry holding just that date and the account or device identifier, with no name, email, or content. These entries are used only for those aggregate charts and are removed automatically after 800 days (about 26 months). They aren't removed early when you delete your account or sign in on a device that was signed out. There's no setting to turn this off, because nothing beyond this is collected; clearing the app's storage gives you a fresh anonymous identifier from then on.

Analytics

We do not collect analytics, telemetry, or crash reports from this app, and do not track what you do inside it. The only usage figures it keeps are the two uses of the anonymous per-device identifier described above: counting an ad's unique viewers (see "Publishing as an ad"), and the activity counts (see "Activity counts").
