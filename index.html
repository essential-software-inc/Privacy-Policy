Privacy Policy

Effective 28 September 2026

Bluebook is published by Essential Software. This policy describes what data the app stores, where it lives, and what leaves your device.

On-device storage

All pages, drafts, images, favourites, preferences, and UI state are stored locally on your device, with one exception: which stories you've opened is tracked server-side against your account when you're signed in, not stored on-device (see "Stories, subscriptions & likes" below). For each account signed in on the device, the app also keeps a copy of which notes you've liked and which accounts you follow (signing out clears the follow copy), a short queue of any likes or follows you tapped while offline that haven't been sent yet, and a small number recording how far your device's clock is from Essential Software's servers, used to time-stamp those taps correctly. If you sign in with Google, your notes, along with your favorites and theme choice, are additionally backed up to Essential Software's servers — see below.

Google sign-in & sync

Signing in is optional. It's handled by Google — Essential Software never sees your Google password. Essential Software receives your Google account identifier and email address, and stores them along with when you signed up, when you last signed in, and a session token so you stay signed in (a session lasts up to 30 days). While signed in, your notes (including embedded images), along with which notes you've marked as favorites and your theme choice, are backed up to Essential Software's servers so they can be restored on another device, and your Pro purchase (if any) and any ad view-credit balance — see "In-app purchases" below — are linked to your account so they carry over too. Signing out stops further syncing but does not delete the backup already stored — sign back in to pick it up again.

You can delete your account at any time by tapping the account icon and choosing Delete account under Show more. Doing so signs this device out and schedules your account for deletion in 30 days. When that time comes, Essential Software permanently deletes your account record, synced notes and images, profile name and picture, subscriptions (both ones you made and ones to you), story-seen, like and follow records (including the timestamps kept for them), running ads and their view statistics, and any unspent ad view-credit balance, and takes down every page you published (a copy of each page is then kept for up to 30 more days, as described under "Publishing a page"). Until then your published pages and stories stay online. Signing back in with the same Google account during the 30 days cancels the deletion and restores full access. Every device where you're signed in is signed out as part of the deletion (each one finds out the next time the app contacts the server, usually within about a minute). Deleting your account does not delete anything held by Google or RevenueCat, which is governed by their own policies.

In-app purchases

Unlocking Pro and buying ad view-credit packages are both purchases made through Google Play Billing, processed on Essential Software's behalf by RevenueCat, a third-party subscription-management service. RevenueCat receives the purchase and entitlement data needed to confirm what you bought, tied to an ID: before you sign in that's an anonymous ID RevenueCat generates for your install, and once you sign in it's your Google account identifier, which is what lets a purchase be carried over to another device. The two unlocks work differently after that: whether Pro is active is checked directly against RevenueCat by the app itself each time, not through Essential Software's own servers. Ad view-credits do involve Essential Software's servers, which receive confirmation from RevenueCat — not from Google directly — and credit the views to your account. Essential Software never receives your payment details, which Google handles directly. See RevenueCat's own privacy policy for how it handles what it receives.

Publishing a page

Publishing is optional — it only happens when you tap Publish. Your page's content, its title, description and tags (if you've added them), when the note was created, and the address you choose are sent to Essential Software and stored so the page can be served at a public link. Anyone with that link can view the page. Essential Software also gives you a private token for that page. It's stored on your device, not kept as-is on Essential Software's side — only a scrambled version of it is kept there, just enough to confirm it matches when you send it back to update or unpublish. Updating a published page sends the new content the same way. Unpublishing takes it down, and if the page was running as an ad, the ad stops too. A copy may be kept for up to 30 days afterward in case of an abuse report or takedown request, then deleted. Publishing also briefly checks your IP address (for about a minute) to limit spam, without storing it. Separately, likes, subscribing, marking stories as seen, and backups are rate-limited with a short-lived counter tied to your account, which expires within a couple of minutes.

Publishing as an ad

From the Publish sheet's "Publish as Ad" tab, you can additionally register an already-published page to run as an ad, shown to other people using this app as a starting template when they tap Create — not only to people who have the page's link, which is how a regular published page works. Running an ad requires signing in. Buying views is a purchase handled as described under "In-app purchases" above; once confirmed, Essential Software credits the corresponding number of views to your account. Essential Software also stores how many views an ad has used and its current status (running, used up, or taken down). To also report how many unique viewers an ad has reached, alongside its total view count, Essential Software stores an anonymous identifier generated and kept on each device — sent whenever a view is counted, whether or not the viewer is signed in. It isn't linked to a Google account, email address, or any other identifying information, and exists only to tell whether a repeat view of a given ad came from a device already counted toward that ad; see "Analytics" below. Unpublishing an ad removes it from Create immediately and forfeits any views it had left — that balance is not restored to your account. The underlying published page and its normal link are unaffected by unpublishing an ad; taking the page itself down is still done separately from the Publish to Web tab. See "External images & embeds" below for how images, video, audio, and social-post embeds inside an ad are fetched — ads are additionally restricted to images from Wikipedia or Wikimedia Commons, and to video, audio, and social-post embeds added through the editor's own tools (YouTube, Instagram, or Spotify).

Reporting a published page

Anyone can report a published page from its public link. Reporting checks the reporter's IP address for about a minute, just to stop spam, then discards it — it's never stored as part of the report itself. Essential Software keeps only the reason and details submitted, along with which page it's about and when. Each report is kept on file so it can be reviewed, and may also be forwarded as a notification to Essential Software's private alerting channel. Reports of child sexual abuse material are handled as the law requires, including being passed to the proper authority.

This applies the same way to pages currently running as ads — an ad and its published page are the exact same thing at the exact same web address, so there's only one way to report either: go to that page's address and use the report link there. There's no separate process for ads.

Right now, the app doesn't show you that address while an ad appears as a starting point on the Create screen. To find it: without adding anything new to it, save the ad and open the Publish sheet for that note — it will automatically fill in the ad's own web address for you. Open that address in a browser and you'll see the report link.

External images & embeds

Images embedded in a page via URL are fetched directly from their source when the page is built. YouTube, Instagram, and Spotify embeds load directly from those services. Essential Software has no involvement with these third-party hosts. A page published as an ad is additionally restricted to images linked directly from Wikipedia or Wikimedia Commons, and cannot contain images uploaded from your device — this is checked automatically before an ad can go live or be updated.

Linked pages

This section covers links inside the app. Tapping a link in a page shows its address in a sheet with a copy option. The app does not open, load, or navigate to the linked page — nothing is fetched, and Essential Software has no visibility into what links a page contains. A published page works differently, and how you're viewing it matters: someone who opens its public web address directly, outside the app, is looking at a plain web page in their own browser, so links inside it are ordinary clickable links that open like any other link on the web. Within the app itself, opening a story or a note found through search shows that same published page in-app, in the app's own built-in viewer (see "Stories, subscriptions & likes" below) — you don't leave the app to see it. A link tapped inside that in-app view currently does nothing — the app has no handler wired up to open it, so the tap is silently ignored rather than opening anywhere.

Stories, subscriptions & likes

From a published note's Publish sheet, you can turn on "Show in stories" (this requires signing in). Doing so adds the note to a stories strip shown to other people using the app — not only to people who have the link — similar to how Publish as Ad works, but without purchased views. Essential Software's servers store the note's web address, title, description and tags (if you've added them), when the note was created, and up to three preview images extracted from the note's published page (plus how many images the note has, so a feed card can show how many more there are), along with your account identifier. The title is shown on your story; the title, description, tags and how long ago the note was created are shown on the note's card in the Subscribed feed of anyone who subscribes to you. Preview images that come from your device are copied to Essential Software's servers and can be loaded without a sign-in check while the story is showing; a preview image added by URL isn't copied, so viewers' devices load it directly from its source when they see your story or feed card, and that host can see their IP address. Turning the toggle off or unpublishing the note deletes the stored copies, including the stored description and tags. Other people's devices never receive your Google account identifier or email. Instead, each account is given a separate random author ID that means nothing outside the app; it's sent with your stories so your picture and display name can be tied to them and a Subscribe tap can reach the right account. It's generated by Essential Software, isn't derived from your Google account, and is deleted with your account. What a viewer sees to tell you apart from another author is your display name, picture, or the note's title. A story appears in the strip for 24 hours; it stays in the Subscribed feed of people who follow you (from when they followed you onward) until you turn the toggle off or unpublish the note. Turning the toggle off removes the note from the strip going forward.

A display name and profile picture are both optional. A display name, if set, is shown to other people on your stories and, to anyone who subscribes to you, on your notes in their Subscribed feed; leaving it blank means viewers just see the note's title instead, in both places. A display name is capped at 30 characters. A profile picture, if set, is shown only on your stories, as the ring avatar in the stories strip — it does not appear on your notes in the Subscribed feed. A profile picture you upload is stored on Essential Software's servers and served back from a web address keyed to your random author ID. That address isn't behind a sign-in check, but it only serves your picture while you have at least one story showing (or to you, when you're signed in) — so anyone who has your author ID, for instance from seeing one of your stories, can load it during that time, and no one else can. Once you have no stories showing, the picture is no longer served to others. Removing the picture deletes it from Essential Software's servers; leaving it unset (or removing it) means the story ring falls back first to the note's own image if it has one, and only then to an initial letter — taken from your display name, or the note's title if that's unset too. That initial is never taken from your account email, which is never sent to other viewers at all.

A signed-in account can subscribe to (follow) another account from that account's story. Essential Software stores who follows whom so it can build your Subscribed feed and so an account can tell it's already following another. You can see how many accounts follow you, as a total, not who; the app also keeps a local copy of who you follow on your device to show the Subscribe/Subscribed button without a round trip. Essential Software also records, tied to your account, which stories you've opened, so a story you've already seen doesn't keep drawing your attention in the strip. Searching your Subscribed feed happens on your device, against the stories already loaded; what you type is not sent to Essential Software. A signed-in account can also like a published note; Essential Software records, tied to your account, which notes you've liked so the heart shows correctly across your devices. For each note you like or unlike, and each account you follow or unfollow, it also keeps the time of your most recent such action, so that taps made on different devices (or offline and sent later) are applied in the right order; that timestamp remains after you unlike or unfollow and is removed once the like or follow is gone and the timestamp is older than 30 days, or when your account is deleted. A note's author is shown only one total, the number of likes across all their published notes, not who liked them or which notes they're on. Unpublishing a note removes the likes it received, so that total goes down accordingly.

Third-party requests & server logs

Using the app makes some requests to third parties, which see your IP address like any website you visit: a script library (three.js) is loaded from Cloudflare's cdnjs when the app opens; Google's sign-in code is loaded from Google when you sign in; and the app occasionally requests a tiny blank page from Google (gstatic.com) just to check whether you're online. Requests to Essential Software's servers run on Cloudflare, which processes them and may keep standard request logs (such as time, address requested, and IP address) that Essential Software can use to operate and debug the service. Essential Software doesn't use them for analytics or tracking.

Analytics

Essential Software does not collect analytics, telemetry, or crash reports from this app. The one exception is the anonymous per-device identifier described under "Publishing as an ad" above: it exists only to count an ad's unique viewers, isn't used to track app usage generally, and doesn't apply anywhere outside that feature.
