Bluebook Privacy Policy

Effective 3 November 2026

Bluebook is published by Essential Software (“we”, “us”, and “our” in this policy), and we're glad you're here. This page explains in plain language what the app stores, where it lives, and what leaves your device.

On-device storage

Your pages, drafts, images, favourites, playlists, preferences and app settings all stay on your device. There's one exception: when you're signed in, we keep track of which stories you've opened on our servers, under your account (see "Stories, subscriptions & likes" below). For each account signed in on the device, the app also keeps a copy of which notes you've liked and which accounts you subscribe to (signing out clears the subscription copy). It keeps a short queue of any likes or subscriptions you tapped while offline that haven't been sent yet. If you sign in with Google, your notes, along with your favourites, playlists, theme choice and your "All my notes" switches from Settings, are also backed up to our servers (see below). Those switches are kept on your device separately for each account, so one account's choices never touch another account's pages, even on a shared phone.

App lock

App lock is optional, except that an author has to turn it on before they can add or remove editors or restore an earlier version of a shared page (see "Sharing a note with editors (Teams)" below). If you turn it on, Bluebook asks for a PIN or password whenever you open the app fresh or refresh the page, and again when you come back after being away for more than 30 seconds, so other people can't peek at your notes.

Your PIN or password is never saved the way you typed it. The app turns it into a scrambled code (called a hash) and keeps only that code, on your device. When you type it later, the app scrambles what you typed and checks whether the two codes match. Nothing about your lock is sent to us or saved to your account, so we can't see it, reset it or recover it for you.

When you set it up, you also pick a recovery question and write an answer. The question is kept as plain text on your device so the app can show it to you if you forget your PIN. The answer is scrambled the same way as your PIN. Pick something only you know, because anyone who gets the answer right can get in.

If you type a wrong PIN or a wrong answer five times in a row, the app makes you wait before you can try again. The wait starts at 30 seconds and grows up to 15 minutes. It's there to slow down anyone who's guessing.

If you forget both your PIN and your recovery answer, we can't get you back in, and we can't reset it for you. The only way past it is to clear Bluebook's data in your phone or browser settings. Notes you backed up by signing in can be restored afterwards, but anything that only lived on this device is gone. So pick a PIN and answer you'll remember, and keep backups.

One last thing: app lock is like a door lock for the app. It doesn't scramble the notes stored on your device. For extra privacy, keep your phone's own screen lock on too.

Google sign-in & sync

Signing in is totally optional, and Google handles it, so we never see your Google password. We get your Google account ID and email address, and Google also tells us whether it has checked that email. We keep them along with your sign-up date, your last sign-in date, the last time the app was active on your account (see "Activity counts" below), and a code that keeps you signed in. That code lasts up to 30 days. While you're signed in, we back up your notes (including embedded images and voice notes) to our servers so you can restore them on another device. We also back up your favourites, your playlists (their names and the songs and videos you put in them), your theme choice and your "All my notes" switches (see "All my notes" below). Your Pro purchase (if you have one), any ad view credit balance and your page space balance (see "In-app purchases" below) are linked to your account too, so they come with you. Signing out stops syncing. Your backup stays put, so you can pick it up again when you sign back in, and deleting your account removes it, as described below. If an image in your backup isn't used by any note any more (for example, after you delete a note or swap an image), we delete it once it's more than 14 days old.

Your account is yours to keep or remove. You can delete it any time by tapping the account icon and choosing Delete account under Show more. That signs this device out and schedules your account for deletion in 30 days. It can take up to a day longer than the 30 days. When it does, we permanently delete your account record and your backup: your synced notes, images, voice notes and playlists. We also delete your display name and profile picture, your subscriptions (the ones you made and the ones to you), the records of the stories you've opened, and your likes and subscriptions (including the times we noted for them). Your running ads and their view statistics go too, along with any unspent ad view credit balance, your page space balance and its list, your invite code and any invites you made or used, and your ad access record. Last, we delete your team memberships, the list of people you've blocked from inviting you, your "Invitations to edit" choice, and your alerts (including ones you deleted from your list). In team chats, we delete your voice messages and images, your reactions and your read markers. Your text messages and summaries stay for the team with your name taken off, and if you were the author of a team, its whole chat is deleted (see "Messages (team chat)" above). We also take down every page you published (we keep a copy of each page, along with its record, for about 30 more days, as described under "Publishing a page"). Until then, your published pages and stories stay online. Changed your mind? Sign back in with the same Google account during the 30 days and the deletion is cancelled, with full access restored. Every device where you're signed in gets signed out as part of the deletion. Deleting your account doesn't delete anything held by Google or RevenueCat. Their own policies cover that.

In-app purchases

Unlocking Pro, buying view packages and buying page space packs are all purchases made through Google Play Billing. RevenueCat, a third-party subscription service, handles them for us. RevenueCat gets the purchase details it needs to confirm what you bought, linked to an ID. Before you sign in, that's a temporary ID RevenueCat makes for your install. Once you sign in, it's your Google account ID, which is what lets a purchase follow you to another device. Whether Pro is active is checked with RevenueCat, and the answer is kept on your device. We add the view credits you buy to your account. We keep a list of your view credits, and every entry has the time. The list shows each purchase (for a particular ad, or added to your balance from Settings), each time views are put on an ad (with that ad's address), each refund or return, and each gift of free views for an invite. Views returned to you stay in your balance, and you can put them on an ad without buying again. That doesn't involve Google Play or RevenueCat. If Google Play refunds a purchase, we take those views back. That list is deleted with your account. Page space works the same way. We add the space you buy to your page space balance and keep a record of it: your purchases, the pages it is used on, and any refunds. If Google Play refunds a pack, we take that space back. That record is deleted with your account. We never get your payment details, because Google handles those directly. See RevenueCat's own privacy policy for how it handles what it receives.

Publishing a page

Publishing is always your choice, and it only happens when you tap Publish. We receive your page's content, its title, description and tags (if you've added them), when the note was created, and the address you choose, and we store them so the page can be shown at a public link. Anyone with that link can view the page. If you've given the note a category, we save that too. It's just your own private label, so it doesn't show on the page or on any card. We only send it back to you when you're signed in, so if you ever restore a page as a note, it goes back into the right category. You can publish without signing in, and then the page isn't linked to any account. If you later sign in and, using that page's private token on your device, turn on "Show in stories" or save an update to it, we link the page to your account from then on. It then shows up among your published pages, counts towards your account's page limit, and is taken down if you delete your account. We also give you a private token for each page. It's stored on your device, and we don't keep it in its original form. We only keep a scrambled version, just enough to check that it matches when you send it back to update or unpublish. Updating a published page sends the new content the same way. Unpublishing takes the page down, and if it was running as an ad, the ad stops too. We may keep a copy for about 30 days afterward, in case of an abuse report or takedown request, and then delete it. If the page was reported as child sexual abuse material, we keep the copy for 18 months instead (see "Reporting a published page"). The page's own record is kept for the same time and then deleted. It holds the address, a scrambled form of the private token, the title, description, tags and category, and, if the page was linked to an account, that account's Google account ID. That's why it stays even after the account itself is deleted. If we remove a page because of a takedown request or abuse report, its address is reserved permanently. To keep that reservation in place, we keep a small record for as long as the address stays reserved. It holds the address, a scrambled form of the private token, the title, description, tags and category, and, if the page was linked to an account, that account's Google account ID. This record stays even after the copy or the account is deleted, because deleting it would lift the reservation. Publishing also briefly counts requests from your IP address to limit spam. That count is only needed briefly. We then delete it, so it's gone within a day, and it isn't linked to your account or the page. Separately, to stop spam, we also count how often you like, comment, subscribe, mark stories as seen, add an invite code, change your privacy choices, send a text box message as yourself, invite or manage editors, or back up. That count is linked to your account. We only need it for a short time. We then delete it, so it's gone within a day.

Page space

Page space lets you publish pages bigger than the free size. It belongs to your account and isn't tied to one page. The Consumer Terms explain how it works.

Large pages are uploaded in stages. If an upload doesn't finish, we keep what was sent for a short time so you can carry on, then delete it.

Reserved names and verified ticks

Sometimes a person asks us to keep a page address safe just for them, like their name. We call this a reserved name, and we only do it when someone asks, for example by emailing us. To set one up, we save the address, the email address the person gave us, the date we sent the invite, and the date it runs out, which is 90 days later. We also save which of our team set it up, and a short note if they wrote one. We need all of that to keep the right name for the right person.

Here's how an invite works. The person signs in with Google using that same email, then publishes a page at the reserved address. The first time they do, the name becomes theirs. We then save their Google account ID and the date, so we know it belongs to them. From then on, only that account can publish to that address. If the page is unpublished, the name stays theirs for 30 days, and we send them alerts before it's freed up. We only do this when Google tells us it has checked the email, and we remember that answer next to the account.

A page on a verified name shows a small tick at the bottom, under the “Report an issue” link. A page by someone who holds a verified name, but that isn't on that name, shows a ring tick instead. The tick also shows next to the author's name on their cards in other people's feeds, liked lists and stories, in the same two looks: a filled tick for a page on a verified name, and a ring tick for a page by someone who holds one. If nobody uses an invite within 90 days, we cancel it, and we delete the record. If an account is deleted, or the page stays unpublished for 30 days, we free up its name, delete the record, and the tick goes away too. If you'd like a name or an email removed sooner, just ask us (see "Contact") and we'll sort it out.

Using published pages and comments

We may use the pages you've published, and the comments on them, to run, build, test and improve Bluebook and our other apps, including by training computer programs (this is called machine learning). This only covers published pages and their comments, which anyone who opens the page can already see. It doesn't cover notes that are only backed up to your account.

If you live somewhere with privacy laws, like the EU or the UK, we do this because we have a good reason to improve our apps (the law calls this a "legitimate interest"). You can say no any time. Choose "No, keep out" when we ask the first time you sign in, or change your choice any time in Settings under Privacy, and from then on we stop using your published pages, and the comments you write, for this. It covers everything on your account, old and new. Pages you published without signing in aren't linked to an account, so the switch can't reach them, but you can unpublish them. The page or comment itself stays where it is unless you unpublish or delete it.

There are limits to what we can undo. If we've already used a page or comment to build or train something, we may not be able to take it back out. And we can't control copies other people have already made, like a page someone saved or shared.

Comments

If a page's author turns on comments, people who are signed in can comment on it and reply to each other. For each comment we save what it says, when it was written, whether it's a reply, your Google account ID and your author ID. Comments show up on the live page for anyone who opens it. Normally a comment shows your display name and profile picture, or "Anonymous" if you haven't set a display name. If you'd rather not be named, go to Settings, under Privacy, and set "Appear as" to Anonymous. Then your comments show as Anonymous, with no name or picture, and the alerts they send say "Someone" instead of your name. This choice is saved with your account, so it's the same on every device you sign in on. It covers all your comments, old and new. It also decides how you appear anywhere else a name can show: in team chats and team lists, in team invitations and alerts, and on text box messages you send as yourself. So if you switch back to your name and picture, they'll show on all your comments again. Changing your display name changes it on all of them too, unless you're appearing as Anonymous.

Anonymous only hides you from other people. We can still tell which account wrote each comment. That lets you delete your own comments, lets replies reach you, and lets us act on comments that break the Consumer Terms. If you're the author of the page, your comments on it still show a tag saying they're from the author. Signed-in people can also like a comment, but not their own. For each like we save which account liked which comment and when, so the heart shows correctly and a comment can't be liked twice. Everyone sees how many likes a comment has, never who liked it, and the comment's author gets an alert that says "Someone" liked it. Unliking removes the like, and deleting a comment, unpublishing its page or deleting an account removes the likes it involves. We keep comments until you or the page's author deletes them, the page is unpublished, or your account is deleted. When a page's comments load or refresh, we also briefly count requests from your IP address to stop spam, the same way we do when you publish. We only need that count briefly. We then delete it, so it's gone within a day, and it isn't linked to any comment or to your account.

Polls

A page's author can add a poll to a published page. A poll is a question people can vote on. The author can write their own answers, or pick Yes / No, or a rating from 1 to 5 or 1 to 10. Anyone who opens the page can vote. They don't need to sign in, and a vote isn't linked to an account.

When you vote, your device sends us the page's address, which poll it is, the answer or answers you picked, and a poll code. Your device makes that code and keeps it in the browser's storage. It's how we know this device has already voted, so the poll can show your choice back to you. We only keep a scrambled version of the code, mixed with the page and poll it belongs to, so it can't be used to spot you on other pages. We save it with your answers and the time you voted. We also keep how many votes each answer got, in total and for each day, so the author can see totals. None of this is linked to a Google account, email address, name, or anything else that identifies you. Because the code belongs to a device, someone who clears their browser storage, or votes from another device, looks like a new voter. So a poll is a friendly guide to what people think, not an exact count. When a page loads its poll, or you vote, we also briefly count requests from your IP address to stop spam, the same way we do when you publish. We only need that count briefly. We then delete it, so it's gone within a day, and it isn't linked to anything.

What people see depends on what the page's author picks. They can show results after someone votes, always, or never. They can let people change their vote, set a close date, and close or reopen the poll whenever they like. The author only sees totals: how many people voted, how many votes each answer got, and, on a rating poll, the average rating. They never see who voted for what.

Votes are never part of the published page itself. We keep them until the author resets them, switches the poll to a different type (single choice, multiple choice, Yes / No, or rating), or changes a rating poll between 1 to 5 and 1 to 10 (all of these clear every vote). If the author takes away an answer, that answer's votes are cleared the next time they publish, and anyone who only picked it can vote again. If the page is unpublished or taken down, or its author's account is deleted, the poll stops working and we keep its votes for about 30 days, then delete them. If the same poll is put back on the page within that time, its votes come back. The poll's question and answers are part of the page, so "Using published pages and comments" above covers them. Votes aren't part of that. Polls can't be used in ads.

Text boxes

A page's author can add a text box to a published page. A text box is either a message box, where people write one message, or a form with a few fields, like a name, an email address, and a message. Whatever people type is sent to the page's author. Anyone who opens the page can send one. They don't need to sign in, and a message isn't linked to an account unless the sender chooses “Send as me” (explained below).

When you send something, your device sends us the page's address, which text box it is, and what you typed into each field. We save that with the time you sent it. We don't save a device ID, and we don't link it to a Google account, name, or anything else, unless you type it yourself or you choose “Send as me” (see the next paragraph). That means whatever you type, like your name or email address, is saved and the page's author can read it, so only type what you're happy for them to see. Please don't send passwords, payment card numbers, ID numbers, or other sensitive details through a text box. If you're signed in to the app, you'll see a “Send as me” switch, and it's off until you turn it on. If you turn it on, we also save your display name, whether you have a profile picture, and your author ID with that message, and the page's author sees your name and picture next to it. If you leave it off, none of that is saved. Send as me uses the same “Appear as” choice in Settings, so it isn't available if that's set to Anonymous or you haven't set a display name. If you later change your display name, messages you sent as yourself show the new one. If you set your account to appear as Anonymous or clear your display name, your name and picture are hidden on those messages, and they show again if you switch back or set a name. While they're hidden, we keep your author ID on the message so that can happen, and the page's author can't see it. If you delete your account, you're taken off those messages completely. The message itself stays with the page's author, who can delete it. When a page loads its text box, or you send, we also briefly count requests from your IP address to stop spam, the same way we do when you publish. We only need that count briefly. We then delete it, so it's gone within a day, and it isn't linked to your message. We also keep a count for each text box so it doesn't get swamped. We only need that for a short time, and we wipe it within a day.

The page's author reads what people send inside the app, and only they can delete a message. If they're signed in, we also send them an alert when a new message arrives. The alert says which page it's on, and the sender's name if they sent it as themselves, but not what the message says. We also send the author an alert when a text box is nearly full, when it's full, or when it's getting so many messages an hour that some people are being turned away. Those messages aren't saved. An alert is saved as it was written, so a later name change or switch to Anonymous doesn't change it. Other visitors never see messages. The author is responsible for what they do with what you send them, so if you want a message deleted, ask the author.

Messages are never part of the published page itself. We keep them until the author deletes them, one at a time or all at once. A text box holds up to 5,000 messages, and after that it stops taking new ones until the author deletes some. If the text box is taken off the page, the page is unpublished or taken down, or its author's account is deleted, the text box stops working and we keep its messages for about 30 days, then delete them. If the same text box is put back on the page within that time, its messages come back. The text box's title and field labels are part of the page, so "Using published pages and comments" above covers them. Messages aren't part of that. Text boxes can't be used in ads.

Payments

A page's author can add a payments block to a published page. It lets visitors send them money, like a tip or a bit of support, without leaving the page. To do this, the author pastes in a Stripe Buy Button from their own Stripe account or, once it's available, connects their own Stripe account. Stripe is a company that handles online payments. Anyone who opens the page can pay if they want to. They don't need to sign in.

Some pages use a Stripe Buy Button instead. On those pages your browser loads Stripe's code and button as soon as the page opens, even if you never pay, so Stripe can see your IP address and some details about your device, like on any site that uses Stripe. When you pay, you pay on Stripe. We don't see or save that payment, and the page only holds the author's public Stripe button details. The author sees the payment in their own Stripe account, not through us.

If you pay, you type your card details into a box that Stripe shows inside the page. Your card details go straight to Stripe. They never pass through our servers, so we never see them and we never save them. To start a payment, we tell Stripe the amount you picked, the currency, and which page and block it's for. Stripe may ask you for other things too, like your email address for a receipt, and it looks after those under its own rules. See Stripe's own privacy policy. Stripe may tell us a few details when your payment finishes, but we only keep what's listed in the next paragraph.

When a payment finishes, we save the page's address, which payments block it was, the amount, the currency, the time, and Stripe's reference numbers for that payment. If some or all of it is refunded, we save how much was given back. We don't save your name, email address, card details, device ID or Google account, and we don't link the payment to you. The page's author sees a list of amounts, times and refunds in the app, plus the totals. They don't see who paid in the app. But the money goes into their own Stripe account, and Stripe may show them more about who paid there. That's between them and Stripe, so only pay people you trust.

If you're an author and you connect Stripe (an option that isn't open yet), we save your Stripe account's ID next to your Bluebook account, along with whether that Stripe account is ready to take payments and when you connected it. Stripe checks who you are and pays you out. We never see your ID documents or your bank details, and we never hold your money. It goes straight from the visitor to your Stripe account. We also save your block's settings, like the amounts you picked, the currency, and whether payments are paused. If you delete your Bluebook account, we remove the link to your Stripe account. The Stripe account itself stays yours, and Stripe's own policy covers what it keeps.

When a page loads its payments block, or you start a payment, we briefly count requests from your IP address to stop spam, the same way we do when you publish. We only need that count briefly. We then delete it, so it's gone within a day, and it isn't linked to anything. We also keep a count for each payments block so it doesn't get swamped. We only need that for a short time, and we wipe it within a day.

Payments are never part of the published page itself. If the payments block is taken off the page, the page is unpublished or taken down, or its author's account is deleted, the block stops working and we keep its settings for about 30 days, then delete them. We keep the small payment records (the amount, currency, time and reference numbers) after that, because they're the author's payment history and they don't hold your name, email address or card details. The block's title and words are part of the page, so "Using published pages and comments" above covers them. Payment records aren't part of that.

Sharing a note with editors (Teams)

An author can let other people edit a page they've published. They invite someone by that person's invite code, and the person gets an alert that says who invited them (or says "Someone" if they appear as Anonymous) and lets them accept or decline. If they accept, the app copies the published page onto their device as a note they can edit, and saving it updates the published page for everyone on the team. The author also gets an alert when an editor joins, leaves or saves a change. It shows the editor's name, or just says "Someone" or "An editor" if they appear as Anonymous. We save the page's address, the author's account, the invited account, whether the invitation is pending or accepted, when it was sent, accepted and last used to edit, and a scrambled (hashed) copy of the editor's own edit key. Invitations expire after 7 days, and a page can have up to 5 editors, counting people who've been invited but haven't answered yet. An invitation nobody answers is deleted 30 days after it expires, unless the author cancels it first.

Each time an editor or the author saves a page that has a team, we keep the version it replaces, up to the last 10 (only the last 5 for a very big page), so the author can go back to one. We also write down which account made each save, and when, so the author can see who saved what. If someone appears as Anonymous, we don't show their name. Editors can't change a page's payments, remove its polls or forms, or change a poll's options. The author can remove an editor at any time, and an editor can leave. Unpublishing the page, or deleting the author's account, ends the team and deletes the saved versions, the team's chat and the choice about who can see the team. If the last editor leaves or is removed, the saved versions stay so the author can still go back to one, until the page is unpublished. Deleting an editor's account removes them from every team, and their name comes off the versions they saved too. What happens to their team chat messages is explained under "Messages (team chat)" below. If you decline an invitation, the author isn't told. If you also choose to block the person, we save that choice so they can't invite you again, and any other invitations from them that you haven't answered are withdrawn. The "Invitations to edit" setting lets you stop all invitations (we save that choice too). In Settings, under Privacy, you can see the people you've blocked. Each one shows their display name and picture if they have them, and the date you blocked them. If they have no name, you'll see "Someone". You can unblock people one at a time. Switching it from Nobody back to Everyone lets you clear the whole list at once. Team alerts, such as an invitation, someone joining, leaving or editing, or the author changing who can see the team, are kept like your other alerts. Invitations and these alerts show people's display names, or "Someone" if they have none or appear as Anonymous. Invitations and team lists also show each person's author ID. Our admins can see who is on a page's team and can remove an editor, or cancel an invitation, if the rules require it. If the author or an admin removes you as an editor, you get an alert (an admin's alert gives the reason). If an invitation is cancelled or declined, its alert is deleted. Admins see your display name even if you appear as Anonymous. The author's PIN or password for App Lock, which is asked for when adding or removing editors or restoring an earlier version, stays on their device and never reaches us.

Who can see the team. Normally, an editor can only see who the author is. The author can choose to let editors see the whole team. If they do, every editor can see the name and profile picture of the author and of each editor who has said yes (people who haven't answered yet aren't shown). The author is marked as Author. If someone appears as Anonymous, their name and picture stay hidden. We save the author's choice with the page, send every editor an alert whenever it changes, and delete it when the page is unpublished.

Anyone can find this page. The author can turn this off in Teams. When it’s off, the page doesn’t show up in Stories, in other people’s Subscribed feeds, or in the author’s list of pages or previous and next pages. Only the author and the editors can open its link. Everyone else just sees a “page not found” message. We keep the page’s address, the author’s account and the time they turned it off, so this works. We delete them when the page is unpublished or taken down, or when the account is deleted.

Arrows and page list. Under a published page that’s opened in the app, there are arrows to the author’s earlier and later pages, and a button that lists their pages. The author can hide them in Settings. We keep that choice with their account until they turn it back on or the account is deleted.

Messages (team chat)

Every note with a team has its own team chat. It's like a group chat for the author and the editors of that page. You open it from Messages in the bottom bar, and you need to be signed in. The Messages button shows up while you have a Teams note. If you want it there all the time, turn on "Always show Messages button" in Settings.

In a team chat you can send a text message (up to 2,000 characters), an image, or a voice message (up to 5 minutes). You can reply to a message, and you can add one emoji reaction to it, from a choice of six. When an editor saves a change to a team's page, they can also write a short summary of what they changed (up to 1,000 characters). That's optional. If they write one, it shows up in the chat as a message from them. If they leave it blank, nothing is posted.

Here's what we save. For every message, we save what it says, when it was sent, which account sent it, what type it is (text, image, voice message or summary), and which message it replies to, if any. For an image you upload, we save the picture (the app shrinks it first, and it can be up to 2 MB), its width and height, and its caption. For an image you add by link, we only save the link (it has to start with https://), its width and height, and its caption. We don't make a copy of the picture, so your device loads it straight from the website it lives on, and that website can see your IP address, like any site you visit. For a voice message, we save the recording, how long it is, and a small pattern of bars the app uses to draw the sound wave. For a reaction, we save which account picked which emoji on which message, and when. For a summary, we also save which saved version of the page it goes with.

We also save a read marker for each person in each chat. It's just the time you last opened that chat. The app uses it to count the messages you haven't read and to show the "New messages" line. Nobody else can see it, so there are no "seen" ticks. Chat messages don't send you alerts. Instead, a little number on the Messages button shows how many you haven't read. While you're signed in, the app checks our servers every so often for new messages.

Everyone on the team can read the whole chat, and nobody else can. A message shows the sender's display name and profile picture to everyone on the team, with no story or comment needed, and the author gets an "Author" tag. If your "Appear as" setting is Anonymous, or you haven't set a display name, your messages show as "Anonymous" with a short code and no picture. The code stays the same for you in that chat, so people can tell anonymous teammates apart, but it's different in every other chat. Your "Story picture" setting only changes the ring on your stories, so it doesn't hide your picture here. To hide it, set "Appear as" to Anonymous. The Messages list shows the name of whoever sent the newest message in each chat, or "Someone" if they appear as Anonymous. Anonymous only hides you from your teammates. We still know which account sent each message, so you can delete your own and so we can keep Bluebook safe.

You can delete a message you sent, and the author can delete any message in their team's chat. A deleted message stays in the chat as "Message deleted" for everyone. We wipe what it said, delete its image file or recording, and remove its reactions. We can't take back anything a teammate has already copied or screenshotted. Also, your phone or browser may keep a private copy of images and voice messages you've opened, so they load quickly next time. We can't remove those copies from your device.

Chats don't keep everything forever. Each chat keeps its newest 1,500 messages. When new ones come in, the oldest go, along with their reactions, images and recordings. Until then, the images and voice messages you send stay in the chat. When you scroll through a chat or the Messages list, the app loads it a bit at a time. That just keeps things fast. It doesn't change what we save.

If you leave a team, or the author or an admin removes you, you lose the chat. We delete your reactions and your read marker for that chat, but your messages stay, with your name on them as before. If the author unpublishes the page, or the author's account is deleted, the team ends and we delete its whole chat: every message, image, recording, reaction and read marker. If an editor deletes their account, we delete their voice messages and images (they show as "Message deleted"), plus their reactions and read markers. Their text messages and summaries stay for the team, with their name taken off, so they show as "Deleted account".

If your account is suspended, or it's waiting to be deleted, you can't send, react to or delete messages. To stop spam, we briefly count how fast your account sends messages, voice messages, images and reactions. We only need that count briefly, and it isn't linked to anything in the chat.

For voice messages, the app asks your device for permission to use the microphone, the same as for voice notes. It only records after you press the mic, and it stops after 5 minutes at most. Nothing goes to us until you send the voice message, and you can delete a recording before you send it.

We don't read team chats as part of running Bluebook, and we don't listen to voice messages or turn them into text. Team chats aren't part of "Using published pages and comments" above, so we don't use them to build or train anything. Team chats are also not end-to-end encrypted. They're stored on our servers so everyone on the team can read them, so please don't send passwords, payment card numbers, ID numbers or other sensitive details in one.

Publishing as an ad

Want more people to see a page? From the Publish sheet's "Publish as Ad" tab, you can turn a page you've already published into an ad. Other people using the app see it as a full-screen ad before a note they open, whether it's from the stories strip, Subscribed, Liked, Media or Go to note. It isn't just for people who have the page's link, like a regular published page. Everyone sees ads, including people who've bought Pro, but never before their own notes. So that ads don't come too often, the app keeps a small count of the notes you've opened and the time of your last ad. It stays on your device and we never see it. You need to sign in to run an ad. Views are bought as described under "In-app purchases" above, and once the purchase is confirmed, we add those views to your account. We also save how many views an ad has used and its status (running, used up, or taken down). To tell you how many different people an ad reached, we save a device ID, a random number that each device makes, keeps, and sends whenever it asks for an ad or a view is counted, whether or not the viewer is signed in. It isn't linked to a Google account, email address, or anything else that identifies someone. We use it to tell whether a repeat view of an ad came from a device we already counted, and for the activity counts described under "Activity counts" below. We also use it to see how long we've known a device, and to make sure one device isn't shown too many ads. We keep a small count for that and wipe it within a day. We use a random one-time code to check that each view is real. The code isn't linked to you or your device. We delete it when it's used, or after about six hours if it never is. When the app asks for an ad, we also briefly count requests from your IP address to stop spam, the same way we do when you publish. See "Analytics" below too. When you unpublish an ad, it stops being shown right away, though someone whose phone already loaded it might still see it once. Any views it had left go unused. That balance isn't returned to your account. The page itself and its normal link stay as they were, and taking the page down is a separate step from the Publish to Web tab. "External images & embeds" below explains how images, video, audio and social posts inside an ad are loaded. Ads can use images linked from any website, but not images uploaded from your device. Video, audio and social posts have to be added with the editor's own tools (YouTube, Spotify, Instagram, X, or Streamable). Voice notes can't be used in ads, because they're recorded on your device. Polls can't be used in ads either, because ads can't collect votes.

Making sure ad views are real (Android)

Some people try to fake ad views with scripts. To stop that, the Android app asks Google to confirm that it's the real app, installed from Google Play, on a real phone. When the app needs an ad, it sends Google's answer to our servers, along with a one-time code, the app's name and the same device ID described above. Our servers ask Google if the answer is good, and we don't keep it. If it is, we give the app a pass that lasts about a day, and the app shows that pass each time it asks for an ad. We keep the one-time code for a short time, and the pass (linked to the device ID) for up to about a day. We use them only to limit how often one phone can ask for ads. To help with that, we also briefly count requests for a pass from your IP address, and how many passes each device ID has asked for. We wipe the IP count, and the device count goes after a day. None of this tells us who you are, and it isn't linked to your account.

Ad access & invites

Some bigger ad packages have to be earned. To work out which packages you can start and how many ads you can run at once, our servers count a few things, using the records described in this policy. They count your subscribers, published notes, stories, likes and comments you've received and given, accounts you subscribe to, how many of the last 30 days your account was active, your running ads, and any strikes. A strike comes from an alert we sent you about a removal, pause or suspension. We keep those alerts for up to a year, but a strike only counts for 60 days, or until we excuse it. You see these counts as your progress. We use them to apply the limits, and other people never see them.

Every signed-in account gets a random invite code, saved with the account. When someone adds a code, we save a record of who entered whose code, when it happened, and whether and why it stopped counting (the invited account was suspended or being deleted, or we removed it for abuse). A friend can add one code, in their first 14 days. You get an alert when a friend joins, but it doesn't say who. You only see totals: how many friends added your code and how many count right now, never who. An invite counts once your friend has published a note and been active on at least two days. If your friend chooses to subscribe at the same time, that's saved like any other subscription. When you share your code, it goes through your device's share sheet or clipboard, so the message is sent by the app you pick and we never see it. When an invite counts, we add 100 free views to your balance. We add it to the list of your view credits, together with the invited account's Google account ID, so the same invite can't be rewarded twice. That entry stays in that list (and only there) until you delete your account, even if your friend's account is deleted first. If either account is deleted, the record of the invite itself is deleted.

Reporting a published page

Reports help keep Bluebook a good place for everyone. Anyone can report a published page from its public link. To stop spam, we count how many reports come from an IP address. We only need that count briefly after the first report from that address. We then delete it, so it's gone within a day. It's never saved as part of the report. We only keep the reason and details that were sent, which page it's about, and when. A report doesn't record who sent it. A report stays on file until someone reviews it, even if the page is unpublished or its author's account is deleted in the meantime. After it's handled, it's deleted. The exception is reports of child sexual abuse material. We keep those for 18 months, along with a copy of the reported page. The report's 18 months start when it's closed. The page copy's 18 months start when it was reported, or when it was most recently reported if it's reported again. The same period covers any other copy we hold of that page. We may also send a notification with the page's address, the reason and the details to our private alerts channel. We handle reports of child sexual abuse material the way the law requires, including passing them to the proper authority. We keep a short record of each one we pass on (the page's address, the date, the authority's reference for it, and a brief note) for 18 months from the latest. When we take an action (like a takedown, a dismissed report, or a restored page), we write down the action, the page's address, the time, and a short note such as the reason given. We keep that for about 13 months, as a record of what was done.

Pages running as ads work the same way. An ad and its published page are exactly the same thing at the same address, so there's only one way to report either: go to that page's address and use the report link there. There's no separate process for ads.

An ad you see in the app is just the published page, so it has the same “Report an issue” link at the bottom as any other page. Scroll down to find it. If you run an ad, you can find its address on the Publish sheet for that note.

When admins step in

Sometimes one of our admins has to step in, for example to remove a page, pause an ad, give views back, add or lift a strike, or send an announcement. When that happens, we write a short note about it. It says what was done, which page it was about, the reason, which admin did it, and when. It doesn't include IP addresses, private tokens or email addresses. We delete these notes automatically after about 400 days. They aren't removed early if a page is unpublished or an account is deleted.

Alerts

We want you to know what's happening with your account and the app. When one of your pages is reported, or we remove or pause something of yours, change your account's status, or unpublish a page because a refund took back its page space, you'll get an automatic message (an alert) that says why. Stories, subscriptions & likes

From a published note's Publish sheet, you can turn on "Show in stories" (you need to sign in). That adds the note to a stories strip that other people using the app can see, not only people who have the link. It's similar to Publish as Ad, but without purchased views. Our servers save the note's address, title, description and tags (if you've added them), when the note was created, and your Google account ID. They also save up to three preview images from the note's published page, plus how many images the note has, so a feed card can show how many more there are. Your story shows the title. The Subscribed feed of anyone who subscribes to you shows the title, description, tags, how long ago the note was created, and how many likes it has. Preview images that come from your device are copied to our servers, and they can be loaded without a sign-in check while the story is showing. A preview image added by URL isn't copied, so viewers' devices load it straight from its source when they see your story or feed card, and that host can see their IP address. If you turn the toggle off within the first 24 hours, or unpublish the note at any time, we delete the saved copies, including the saved description and tags. Other people's devices never get your Google account ID or email. Instead, each account has a separate author ID, a random number that means nothing outside the app. It's sent with your stories, so your picture and display name can go with them and a Subscribe tap can reach the right account. We make it ourselves, it isn't made from your Google account, and it's deleted with your account. To tell you apart from other authors, viewers see your display name, picture, or the note's title. A story can appear in the strip for 24 hours from when you turn on "Show in stories" (editing the note doesn't restart that clock). After that, it leaves the strip and your Stories tab. The strip isn't a list of every story. It shows a selection of other people's stories that reshuffles every few hours, so a story still within its 24 hours may or may not be shown to a given viewer. Your own live stories always lead your own strip, and stories from accounts a viewer already subscribes to show up in their Subscribed feed instead of the strip. A story stays in the Subscribed feed of people who subscribe to you (from when they subscribed to you onward) for 7 days from when you turned on "Show in stories", or until you unpublish the note, whichever comes first. After that it leaves the feed, but the published page itself stays online. After the first 24 hours the toggle turns itself off. That just tells you the story has left the strip and your Stories tab. It stays in your subscribers' feeds for the rest of the 7 days. If you turn the toggle off yourself within the first 24 hours, the story disappears from the strip and from your subscribers' feeds.

A display name and profile picture are both optional. If you set a display name, it's shown on your stories and, to anyone who subscribes to you, on your notes in their Subscribed feed. If you leave it blank, viewers just see the note's title in both places. A display name can be up to 30 characters. If you invite someone to edit and they block you, your display name and picture show in their list of blocked people. If "Appear as" is set to Anonymous, they only see "Someone". If "Story picture" is set to never your picture, they won't see your picture. If you set a profile picture, it shows on your stories, as the ring around your profile picture in the stories strip. It also shows next to your name on your comments (see "Comments" below) and on text box messages you send as yourself (see "Text boxes" below). If you're on a team, it also shows beside your messages in the team chat and in the team's list of people (see "Messages (team chat)"). It doesn't show on your notes in the Subscribed feed. You get to pick what your ring shows. In Settings, under Privacy, tap "Story picture" and choose your profile picture (that's the default), the first image in your note, or never your picture. If you pick the note's first image, the ring shows it when the note has one. If not, it falls back to your picture, and then to a letter. If you pick never, the ring shows the note's first image, or a letter if there isn't one, and your picture isn't used in the strip. Your choice is saved with your account, so it works on every device, and it's sent along with your stories so other people's apps follow it. It only changes what the strip shows. It doesn't delete your picture, and it doesn't stop your picture from loading from the address below. It also doesn't hide your picture from your teammates in a team chat. To hide it there, set "Appear as" to Anonymous. A picture you upload is stored on our servers. That address isn't behind a sign-in check. It only serves your picture while you have at least one story showing, or at least one comment or text box message showing your name. It also serves it to you when you're signed in, to someone who blocked you if your settings allow it, and to the people on a team with you (the author and the editors), unless you appear as Anonymous. So anyone who has your author ID, for example from seeing one of your stories or comments, can load it during that time. Apart from you, your teammates and someone who blocked you, no one else can. Once you have no stories, named comments or named text box messages showing, the picture is no longer shown to others, except to your teammates (not if you appear as Anonymous) and to someone who blocked you (and only if your settings allow it). If you remove the picture, it's deleted from our servers. With no picture, or if your choice means it isn't used, the story ring uses the note's own image if it has one, and only then an initial letter, taken from your display name (or the note's title if you haven't set one). That initial is never taken from your account email, which is never sent to other viewers at all.

A signed-in account can subscribe to another account from that account's story. We save who subscribes to whom so we can build your Subscribed feed, show whether you're already subscribed to someone, and keep your subscriptions on every device you sign in on. You can see how many accounts subscribe to you as a total, never who, and how many accounts you subscribe to. The app also keeps a copy of who you subscribe to on your device, so it can show the Subscribe/Subscribed button without asking our servers each time. We also record, under your account, which stories you've opened, so a story you've already seen doesn't keep grabbing your attention in the strip. Searching your Subscribed feed happens on your device, within the stories already loaded. What you type is never sent to us. A signed-in account can also like a published note. We record, under your account, which notes you've liked so the heart shows correctly across your devices. For each note you like or unlike, and each account you subscribe to or unsubscribe from, we also note the time of your most recent action. That way, taps made on different devices (or offline and sent later) are applied in the right order. That note stays after you unlike or unsubscribe, and is removed once the like or subscription is gone and the note is more than 30 days old, or when your account is deleted. Each published note's own like count shows on its card wherever that card appears: your Published and Stories tabs, and the Subscribed feed or Liked tab of anyone who sees the note, but never who liked it. You can also see one total on your account screen: the number of likes across all your published notes. When a published note that isn't showing as a story appears as a card, either in its author's Published tab or in someone's Liked tab, we may copy up to three preview images from that page to our servers. Like story preview images, these copies can be loaded without a sign-in check, and they're deleted when the note is unpublished or its author's account is deleted.

All my notes

In Settings, the "All my notes" section has four switches that change lots of your published pages at once, so you don't have to do them one by one in the Publish sheet. They're "Always show in stories", "Always show comments", "Hide comments on all pages" and "Disable comments on all pages". You need to be signed in to use them. When you turn one on, the app changes every page you've already published, and every page you publish after that. When you turn one off, it just stops changing pages. Your pages keep whatever they're set to at that moment. If you later change a single page's own switches in its Publish sheet, that page's choice wins.

Here's what that means for your data. The three comment switches send a request to our servers, and our servers update the comments setting on every live page linked to your account, even pages that aren't on the device you're holding. It's the same setting you'd get from the Publish sheet, and nothing extra is saved about it. "Always show comments" and "Hide comments on all pages" are opposites, so turning one on turns the other off. "Always show in stories" works one page at a time, exactly like tapping "Show in stories" on each page yourself, so everything described under "Stories, subscriptions & likes" above applies to each page it adds.

The switches themselves (on or off for each of the four, plus the time you last changed them) are saved on your device for your account, and they're backed up with your notes and theme, so they follow you to your other devices. If you change them on two devices, the newest change wins. When another device finds out that "Always show in stories" was turned on, that device's published pages join stories too. Nothing is saved while you're signed out. If you delete your account, these switches are deleted along with the rest of your backup.

Activity counts

We'd like to know roughly how many people use the app, and how many are using it right now, so our servers note when the app is open. While it's open, the app contacts our server when it launches, when you come back to it, and regularly, sending the same device ID described under "Publishing as an ad" above. If you're signed in, this (and any other request you make while signed in) saves only the time your account was last active, on your account. If you're not signed in, the server saves the device ID with the times it was first and last seen (plus the daily log described below), and nothing else. It isn't linked to a name, email address or Google account, and if you later sign in on that device, the signed-out record is deleted. We use these records for private, overall totals (like total, new and currently active users and devices). We also use them to look up an account's last activity when we're running the service. For signed-in accounts, we use them to count recent active days towards ad access and invites (see "Ad access & invites"). They're never shown to other people, shared, or used for advertising. The last-activity record is deleted about 90 days after the last activity (an account's is also deleted with the account). Separately, so usage can be charted across weeks, months and years, the server keeps a minimal daily log. For each day an account or device was active, it holds one entry with just that date and the account or device ID, with no name, email or content. We use these entries for those overall charts and for the active-day counts in "Ad access & invites", and they're removed automatically after 800 days (about 26 months). They aren't removed early when you delete your account or sign in on a device that was signed out. There's no setting to turn this off, because we don't collect anything beyond this. If you clear the app's storage, you get a fresh device ID from then on.

Analytics

We don't collect usage tracking or crash reports from this app, and we don't track what you do inside it. The only usage figures it keeps are the two uses of the device ID described above: counting an ad's unique viewers (see "Publishing as an ad"), and the activity counts (see "Activity counts").
